About Wasan / Timeline

How one book led to the next question.

T

This timeline follows selected works and turning points. Dates identify publications, manuscripts, tablets, or policy changes—not a claim that mathematical practice changed everywhere at once.

  1. 1299China

    Zhu Shijie, Suanxue qimeng

    A major Chinese introduction to arithmetic and algebra. Its later circulation in Japan helped shape early Edo study of equations and computational methods.

  2. 1627Japan

    Yoshida Mitsuyoshi, Jinkoki

    A remarkably successful vernacular arithmetic book whose many editions carried practical calculation and recreational problems to a broad readership.

  3. 1641edition

    Twelve unanswered problems

    An edition of Jinkoki ended with twelve problems but no solutions. This practice of leaving idai helped turn publication into an exchange of challenges.

  4. 1674book

    Seki Takakazu, Hatsubi sanpo

    Seki answered earlier difficult problems and presented sophisticated algebraic procedures, marking a decisive stage in the formation of his mathematical school.

  5. 1685commentary

    Takebe Katahiro, Hatsubi sanpo endan genkai

    Takebe unpacked terse procedures step by step, showing how commentary could make advanced work teachable and preserve its reasoning.

  6. 1683–1711compiled work

    Taisei sankei

    Seki Takakazu and the Takebe brothers participated in the long compilation of a large mathematical synthesis. Individual sections and dates require source-specific care.

  7. 1712book

    Seki Takakazu, Katsuyo sanpo

    Published after Seki’s death, this work includes results on power sums and circle calculations that became important reference points for later scholars.

  8. 1722manuscript

    Takebe Katahiro, Tetsujutsu sankei

    Takebe explored numerical patterns, extrapolation, and circle measurement. Modern interpretations differ on some details, so this site labels reconstruction separately from source text.

  9. 1822sangaku

    Irisawa Shintaro Hiroatsu’s six-sphere chain

    A tablet dedicated at Samukawa Shrine described the configuration now compared with the six-sphere chain, more than a century before Soddy’s related publication.

  10. 1832edited collection

    Uchida Itsumi, Kokon sankan

    Collections and editorial work carried older problems into new contexts and helped preserve a record of practitioners and methods.

  11. 1872education policy

    School policy turns toward Western mathematics

    The new national school system adopted Western mathematics. Wasan did not disappear overnight: practitioners, manuscripts, local teaching, and later historical research continued.

A note on dates: publication, completion, copying, and dedication are different events. Where scholarship disagrees, the relevant page states the uncertainty rather than forcing a single date.
